# Customer Shopping Behavior Analysis

1. Project Overview

This project analyzes customer shopping behavior using transactional data from 3,900 purchases across different product categories.

The objective is to uncover insights into customer spending patterns, customer segments, product preferences, discount usage, and subscription behavior to support strategic business decisions.

The project follows an end-to-end data analytics workflow using Python, PostgreSQL, SQL, Excel, and Power BI.

2. Business Problem Statement

A retail company wants to understand customer shopping behavior to improve sales, customer engagement, customer loyalty, and marketing effectiveness.

The company wants to identify how customer demographics, product preferences, discounts, shipping methods, reviews, and subscription status relate to purchasing behavior and revenue.

Main Business Question:

How can the company use customer shopping data to identify purchasing trends, improve customer engagement, and optimize marketing and product strategies?

3. Dataset Summary

The original dataset contains:

- Rows: 3,900
- Columns: 18
- Missing Data: 37 values in the Review Rating column.

Key Features

- Customer Demographics: Age, Gender, Location, and Subscription Status.
- Purchase Details: Item Purchased, Category, Purchase Amount, Season, Size, and Color.
- Shopping Behavior: Discount Applied, Promo Code Used, Previous Purchases, Frequency of Purchases, Review Rating, and Shipping Type.

5. Project Workflow

A. Data Cleaning and Preparation (Python)

Python and Pandas were used to prepare the dataset for analysis.

Key activities included:

1. Data Loading and Exploration: Imported the dataset and examined its structure using Pandas.
2. Missing Value Treatment: Imputed 37 missing review ratings using the median rating within each product category.
3. Column Name Standardization: Converted column names into lowercase snake_case format.
4. Feature Engineering: Created an "age_group" column to categorize customers into four age groups.
5. Purchase Frequency Transformation: Converted purchase frequency categories into approximate numeric day values.
6. Column Removal: Removed "promo_code_used" from the final analysis dataset.
7. Database Integration: Connected Python to PostgreSQL and loaded the cleaned DataFrame for further analysis.

B. Database Integration (PostgreSQL)

The cleaned dataset was transferred from Python into PostgreSQL using SQLAlchemy.

The data was stored in a table named "customer" and used as the source for SQL business analysis.

C. Data Analysis (SQL)

Ten SQL queries were developed to answer key business questions:

1. Revenue by Gender: Compared total revenue generated by male and female customers.
2. High-Spending Discount Users: Identified customers who used discounts but still spent at or above the average purchase amount.
3. Top 5 Products by Rating: Identified products with the highest average review ratings.
4. Shipping Type Comparison: Compared average purchase amounts between Standard and Express shipping.
5. Subscribers vs. Non-Subscribers: Compared customer counts, average spending, and total revenue by subscription status.
6. Discount-Dependent Products: Identified the five products with the highest percentage of discounted purchases.
7. Customer Segmentation: Classified customers into New, Returning, and Loyal segments based on previous purchases.
8. Top 3 Products per Category: Identified the most purchased products within each product category.
9. Repeat Buyers and Subscriptions: Examined subscription status among customers with more than five previous purchases.
10. Revenue by Age Group: Compared total revenue contributions across customer age groups.

SQL Concepts Demonstrated:

6. Key Business Insights

The analysis explores several important areas of customer shopping behavior:

1. Customer Spending Patterns: Examines purchasing amounts and revenue contributions across customer groups.
2. Subscription Behavior: Compares subscribers and non-subscribers in terms of customer counts, average spending, and revenue.
3. Product Performance: Identifies highly rated products and the most purchased products within different categories.
4. Discount Usage: Examines the relationship between discount usage and customer spending.
5. Customer Loyalty: Segments customers into New, Returning, and Loyal groups based on previous purchases.
6. Demographic Trends: Explores revenue contributions across gender and age groups.
7. Shipping Preferences: Compares average purchase amounts across shipping methods.

7. Business Recommendations

Based on the analytical questions and business objectives, the project proposes the following strategies:

1. Grow Subscriptions: Test clear membership benefits, such as exclusive offers and early access. Monitor subscription rates, repeat purchases, and revenue per customer.

2. Strengthen Customer Loyalty: Use New, Returning, and Loyal customer segments to develop targeted follow-ups and rewards. Monitor repeat-purchase rates and the time between purchases.

3. Optimize Discount Strategy: Identify products frequently purchased with discounts and compare sales and profitability to avoid unnecessary markdowns.

4. Improve Product Positioning: Promote products that combine strong sales performance with high customer ratings. Investigate lower-rated products before increasing promotional activity.

5. Refine Targeted Marketing: Use age-group, category, and gender insights to design targeted marketing campaigns. Compare conversion rates, average order value, and profitability before reallocating marketing budgets.
